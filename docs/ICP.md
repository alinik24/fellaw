# FelLaw ICP

Status: approved direction, PR-00
Updated: 2026-09-09

## End user

Primary: international professionals and other foreign residents who already
live in Germany and receive consequential German legal or administrative
correspondence.

(The age band 20–59 appears ONLY as a market-sizing segment in
MARKET_ASSUMPTIONS.md. It is not a product-eligibility or ICP-exclusion rule.)

Secondary: international university students and researchers.

The end user needs certainty about what arrived, what clock matters, and the
next safe action. They do not need a permanent consumer subscription or a
broad legal-information super-app.

## Initial institutional buyer

- Employers with concentrated international workforces
- Universities and international offices
- Employee-benefit and EAP providers
- Relocation/mobility providers seeking post-relocation support
- Later: Rechtsschutz insurers and assistance networks

The institution supplies distribution, trust, and recurring matter volume. A
person may need this workflow rarely; the institution encounters the burden
continuously.

## Pilot profile

An organization with a meaningful international population, recurring HR or
administrative-support questions, no adequate first-response workflow, and a
willingness to provide professionally reviewed pilot examples without exposing
unnecessary personal data.

## Buying hypothesis

Test a bounded paid pilot at €7,500–€15,000 including onboarding, a defined
notice taxonomy, capped usage, reporting, feedback sessions, and escalation
setup. These are pricing experiments, not established market facts.

## Distribution hypothesis

Start through HR/global mobility, university international offices,
benefits/EAP, and mobility ecosystem partners. Treat Localyze-like providers
as possible distribution or integration partners, not as the category FelLaw
should attempt to replace.

## Explicit exclusions

FelLaw is not initially:

- an immigration or relocation case-management platform;
- a generic consumer legal subscription;
- a law-firm intake/automation product;
- a lawyer directory or marketplace;
- a legal-representation provider without an approved operating model;
- a general legal Q&A or document-generation product.

## Discovery questions

1. How many supported notice events occur per covered population per year?
2. Who currently handles the first response, and at what internal cost?
3. Which documents create the most urgent repeat burden?
4. Will the institution pay for a bounded pilot before broad coverage exists?
5. Does a licensed professional accept the handoff packet without repeating
   the initial intake?
